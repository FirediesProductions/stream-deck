# Focus

**Time you can see without looking away.**

Focus keeps the clock off your screen and on your desk - a pomodoro cycle, a plain countdown, a
stopwatch, and your next meeting with a live countdown to it. One glance, no window to find,
nothing stealing the front of your monitor.

On the timers the bar drains rather than fills, so what you can see is the time you have left. The stopwatch does the opposite - it sweeps and fills, so you can feel the time adding up.

## Quick start

1. Drag **Pomodoro** (under **Focus**) onto any key or dial.
2. Press it. That's a 25-minute focus block running, with a 5-minute break waiting behind it.
3. Open its settings to pick a different rhythm, your own colours, and what it sounds like when a
   phase ends.

## The four

| Action | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Pomodoro** | The focus cycle, with phases and cycle dots so you know where you are. |
| **Timer** | A clean countdown to whatever you set. |
| **Stopwatch** | Counts up. For the things you want to measure rather than limit. |
| **Clock & Calendar** | Your next event and how long until it starts - then a live **NOW** while it runs. |

All four work as a **key** or as a **dial**.

## Why a timer beats willpower

**You don't need more hours. You need the ones you have to stay in one piece.**

Research at the University of California, Irvine measured what an interruption really costs and
found it takes around **23 minutes** to get properly back to the task you left. Three of those in a
morning and the morning is gone - without you doing anything wrong.

A focus block doesn't give you more time. It gives you a **fence**: one task inside it, everything
else waiting outside. And it works in both directions:

- **Starting gets cheap.** "Work on the thing" is open-ended and easy to put off. "Twenty-five
  minutes" is a length you can agree to on a bad day, which is the day it matters.
- **Stopping becomes automatic.** The break isn't a reward for finishing, it's part of the method -
  so you stop before you're empty rather than after.

**How to run one:**

1. Pick *one* thing. Start it.
2. Work on only that until it rings. No inbox, no "quick look".
3. When it rings, **stop** - even mid-sentence - and take the short break.
4. After four rounds, take a long one.

Rule three is the one people fight - and stopping while it's still going well is exactly what makes
you come back *wanting to*. (The name is a tomato, by the way: the inventor used a tomato-shaped
kitchen timer.)

## Six rhythms

| Mode | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Focus** | **25 / 5** - the classic. Short enough to start when you don't want to. |
| **Deep Work** | **50 / 10** - one long stretch, one proper break. |
| **52 / 17** | The ratio from a well-known productivity study, kept because people like it. |
| **Sprint** | **90 / 25** - one full ultradian cycle, for work slow to warm up. |
| **Flow** | Open-ended. It counts up and waits for you to decide you're done. |
| **Adaptive** | You say how big the task is; it sizes the block for you. |

And if none of them is yours, **Custom durations** sets the focus, the short break, the long break,
and how many rounds until the long one.

## The controls

| Gesture | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Press** | Start, or pause. |
| **Hold ½ second** | Skip to the next phase on Pomodoro; restart the Timer; reset the Stopwatch. |
| **Keep holding 2 s** | Start the whole pomodoro over, round count and all. |
| **Turn the dial** | Nudges the running phase by **±10 seconds**, or sets the Timer before you start. |
| **Tap / long-tap** | The two depths again, on a touch strip - though on Pomodoro a tap skips the phase, and on Clock & Calendar a tap joins the meeting. |

A nudge bumps the live phase rather than restarting it, so a meeting that overran doesn't cost you
the block.

## Four looks

**Gradient** (the bar travels between two colours as the phase drains) · **Solid** · **B & W** ·
**Clean** (just the time, large).

## Sound

Eight chimes are included plus two alarms, and any of them can be your own file instead, with its
own volume. There's a separate tactile click on press, also yours to change or switch off.

## Clock & Calendar

Paste your calendar's **secret iCal address** - the *Secret address in iCal format* link in Google
Calendar, or the equivalent in Outlook and Apple Calendar - and the key shows what's next and how
long you have. When the meeting starts it goes to a live **NOW**.

- **More than one calendar** is fine - work and personal together, with an option to merge identical
  events so a shared meeting only appears once.
- **Join the meeting** from the key. Tap the touch strip or hold, and it opens the link out of the
  invitation.
- **Press for a wall clock**, then press again for your events - or set the clock as the default so it is the first thing you see.
  12 or 24 hour, your choice.
- **A strip under the time** - a day arc, plain day progress, or nothing at all.
  Give it your town or postcode and the arc knows your sunrise and sunset.
- **A chime the moment an event starts**, so you never miss the top of a meeting - plus a separate **daily alarm** at a time you set, which can loop until you dismiss it.

## Honest limits

- **The calendar is read-only.** It shows your events and opens their links. It never edits,
  replies, or accepts anything on your behalf.
- **An iCal feed is a snapshot, not a live wire.** Providers refresh these on their own schedule -
  usually minutes, occasionally longer - so an event added seconds ago may not appear immediately.
  That's the provider's cadence, not something we can fix from here.
- **A secret iCal URL is a password.** Anyone holding it can read that calendar. It stays on your
  machine, and if you ever share a screenshot of the settings, blur it.
- **Join only works if the invitation carries a link.** No link in the event, nothing to open.
- **Windows only, for now.**
